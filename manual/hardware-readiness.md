## Verified board-side facts and their limits

1. **Power and assembly:** PDF p20 specifies a DC +5 V board input, an onboard regulator producing +3.3 V for the controller and onboard peripherals, TP1 as +5 V, TP3 as ground, JP6 in the +3.3 V board feed, and TP2 as the +3.3 V test point. These facts do not identify a safe external-module VCC output or available module current. PDF p21 describes the piggyback's CN1–CN4 mounting and calls out “99-05-14 REV00.” The photographs show base `EVBRD/ARMCTXM3TI/BASE`, `REV01` and `99-05-14`, with piggyback `PJTBRD/ARMCTXM3TI/PBAK` and `REV00`. Their designation relationship and fitted processor should be confirmed with a readable underside/chip image or matching schematic.
2. **Connector tables:** PDF p24 documents CNA; p25 documents CNB and CNC; p26 documents CND. All four are 10-pin male box-type FRC connectors. CNC 1 = P0.15/SCK0, 2 = P0.16/SSEL0, 4 = P0.18/MOSI0, 5 = P0.19/SDA1, 6 = P0.20/SCL1, and 10 = ground. These are board-side signal candidates, not voltage or wiring approvals.
3. **Jumpers and sharing:** PDF p25 requires JP4 (1,2) for CNB 8 and JP4 (2,3) for CNC 9; neither condition is a stated prerequisite for the proposed CNC 1, 2, 4, 5, 6 or 10 routes. PDF p26 requires JP5 for CND 4 and says CND 5/6 are unavailable during USB OTG use. It also warns that the GPIO connectors map one-to-one to other board blocks. PDF p35 identifies CNC1 as a compatible interface for onboard U5 SPI EEPROM, U6 RS485 and U16 I²C RTC. If a ribbon joins CNC to CNC1, the corresponding lines can be shared. At CNC1, JP12/JP13 select the SPI or RS485 paths for contacts 1/2. The actual ribbon and jumper states are not established by the photographs.
4. **Placement and labels:** PDF p39 locates connector regions; it does not reliably identify physical contact 1 in the supplied view. The photographs establish the MPU PCB text `MPU-6050`, its header labels `VCC GND SCL SDA XDA XCL AD0 INT`, TFT PCB text `1.8 TFT SPI 128*160 V1.1`, and TFT labels `VCC GND CS RESET A0 SDA SCK LED`. The MPU IC top marking is not legible; the TFT controller is not visible/legible. These labels do not establish regulator, level-shifter, pull-up or backlight circuitry.

## Evidence to obtain, in order

1. **Identify the fitted hardware:** sharp top and underside photos of the trainer base, piggyback, LPC1768 chip marking and revision codes. Obtain the revision-matched base/piggyback schematic to reconcile p21 with the photographed markings.
2. **Identify both modules:** readable front/back photos of all IC and PCB markings, seller/manufacturer link or exact module part number and revision, plus the corresponding schematics/BOMs. The MPU document must show VCC/regulator, I/O levels, SCL/SDA pull-ups and their rail, AD0 bias, and INT/auxiliary-pin circuitry. The TFT document must identify the controller and show VCC, input levels, RESET, A0 and LED/backlight driver/current limit.
3. **Identify the physical connection system:** a close-up showing the trainer CNC key/notch and pin-1 mark, cable ends and stripe/key, the adapter's part number and labelled contact map, and an unpowered continuity record from CNC contacts to adapter terminals. Photograph any existing CNC–CNC1 ribbon.
4. **Record shared paths:** photographs or schematic-backed record of JP6, JP4, JP5, JP12, JP13 and any fitted CNC/CNC1 ribbon. Review U5 EEPROM, U16 RTC and U6 RS485 occupancy (PDF p35), plus the one-to-one routed interface warning (p26). For the proposed contacts, JP4 and JP5 are contextual rather than prerequisites; do not set them from this matrix.
5. **Complete a signed electrical plan:** select a documented, current-capable supply only after the module reviews; show common ground distribution, each signal's source/target level, pull-up rail and combined pin allocation. With the circuit unpowered, record continuity and absence of shorts. Only then can a qualified reviewer separately approve physical wiring. After installation, a user's mark remains unverified until an actual powered test is recorded with firmware, observations and evidence.
